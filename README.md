# English Preparation Blueprint — SBI PO + RBI Grade B

> **Single source of truth for this course.** This file governs the syllabus, priorities, exam-trend interpretation, seven-month plan, daily routine, revision system, and progress rules. If another course file conflicts with this one, follow this README and update the other file.

**Study capacity:** 60 minutes per day  
**Planning horizon:** 7 months  
**Primary method:** PYQ-first, trend-aware, timed practice, spaced revision, and mistake analysis  
**Target exams:** SBI PO (Prelims + Mains) and RBI Grade B (General; English writing paper)

---

## 1. Mission and operating principles

The goal is not to finish an English book. The goal is to become accurate, fast, and adaptable across objective banking-exam English and RBI-style writing.

1. **PYQs before theory overload.** Start with actual or memory-based exam questions. Learn the concept needed to solve them.
2. **Prioritise recurring, transferable skills.** Reading comprehension, grammar in context, vocabulary in context, sentence logic, and clear writing benefit both exams.
3. **Practise under time limits.** Untimed accuracy is the foundation; timed accuracy is the exam skill.
4. **Review every error and every lucky guess.** A correct guess is not mastery.
5. **Use trends as a guide, not a prediction.** Topic counts vary by shift, year, and paper-setter.
6. **Keep objective and descriptive English alive throughout.** Do not postpone RBI writing until the final month.
7. **Measure evidence, not intentions.** A topic is complete only after fresh-question practice and revision.

### Preparation allocation

| Activity | Share | Approx. weekly time |
|---|---:|---:|
| PYQs and exam-level questions | 40% | 168 min |
| Concepts and targeted topic practice | 25% | 105 min |
| Revision and error analysis | 25% | 105 min |
| Vocabulary and editorial reading | 10% | 42 min |

This is a flexible allocation, not a rigid quota. During a weak-topic repair week, temporarily increase concept practice; during mock/consolidation weeks, increase revision and timed sets.

## 2. What the two exams demand

### SBI PO

**Prelims English:** objective questions under a tight sectional time limit. The practical skills are quick passage comprehension, vocabulary from context, grammar-based elimination, and efficient handling of short verbal-ability sets.

**Mains English:** more demanding reading and verbal reasoning, with objective questions plus a descriptive communication paper. Prepare to understand dense passages, identify logical relationships, and write clear, organised responses.

The 2025 SBI PO notification specified 40 English questions in 20 minutes in Prelims. For Mains, it specified a 40-question English objective section and a 30-minute, 50-mark descriptive paper. The 2025 descriptive components were email, report, and either situation analysis or précis. **Always verify the notification for the cycle you apply to; the Bank can change the structure.**

### RBI Grade B — General

The English paper is **descriptive writing**, designed to assess writing skills, expression, and understanding of the topic. It is not simply another bank-English MCQ section.

Build the ability to:
- understand an issue and identify its central argument;
- organise ideas into a logical structure;
- explain causes, consequences, trade-offs, and solutions;
- use precise, formal but natural English;
- summarise without distorting the source;
- type accurately within the allotted time.

The 2026 RBI Grade B General notification describes Phase-II Paper-II as English (Writing Skills), descriptive and typed on a computer, with a 90-minute duration. The notification does not guarantee a fixed essay/précis/comprehension combination. Practise all three, but do not treat any coaching site's predicted format as official.

**Important scope note:** This blueprint covers English for RBI Grade B General. DEPR and DSIM have separate specialist syllabi and should not be treated as interchangeable.

## 3. Recent exam trends and what they mean

### Evidence reviewed

The trend snapshot below uses:
- SBI PO 2025 official notification for the formal structure;
- candidate-feedback/memory-based SBI PO Prelims and Mains analyses from 2025 for observed question types;
- RBI Grade B General 2026 notification for the official descriptive-English requirement.

Memory-based analyses are useful but are not official question papers. A reported shift distribution is one observation, not a guaranteed weightage.

### SBI PO Prelims — 2025 observations

A 4 August 2025 Shift 1 analysis reported a 40-question English section with:
- Reading Comprehension: about 9–10
- Spotting errors: about 6
- Cloze test: about 5
- Para/sentence rearrangement: about 4–5 each in the reported breakdown
- Phrase replacement: about 4
- Word usage: about 3–4
- Fillers, match-the-column, and word rearrangement making up the remaining mix

Other reported shifts also showed RC, cloze, phrase replacement, error detection, and rearrangement/word-order questions. The exact mix differed. The signal is not “memorise these counts”; it is that **RC + contextual vocabulary + grammar + sentence logic** form a high-value preparation core.

### SBI PO Mains — 2025 observations

2025 Mains analyses reported combinations such as:
- multiple RC passages, including passages on medicine/pharma, economy, and social or contemporary themes;
- longer para-jumbles / sentence rearrangement;
- connectors and match-the-column;
- paragraph-level error detection;
- phrase replacement and sentence completion.

The practical implication: Mains preparation must go beyond isolated grammar rules. Practise paragraph coherence, inference, tone, contextual vocabulary, and longer multi-sentence logic.

### RBI Grade B — 2026 official signal

The 2026 notification explicitly frames the General-cadre English paper around writing skills, expression, and understanding of the topic. Therefore, the durable preparation priorities are:
- argument development and essay structure;
- précis and summary writing;
- comprehension and interpretation;
- clarity, grammar, cohesion, and formal expression;
- typing fluency and time management.

Do not rely on an assumed fixed number of essays, précis questions, or comprehension questions unless the current information handout confirms it.

### Trend-based priority ranking

| Priority | Topic cluster | Why it matters | Study treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P1** | Reading comprehension, inference, tone, purpose | Recurs in SBI objective English; builds RBI comprehension and analytical reading | Practise every week |
| **P1** | Grammar in context: error detection, correction, phrase replacement | Recurring objective question family; supports clean writing | Learn rules through PYQs |
| **P1** | Cloze, fillers, contextual vocabulary | Tests meaning, grammar, collocation, and context together | Timed sets + vocabulary log |
| **P1** | Para-jumbles, sentence rearrangement, paragraph coherence | Tests logical flow; useful for both objective and descriptive work | Practise connectors and sentence links |
| **P1** | RBI essay / précis / comprehension writing | Directly relevant to RBI descriptive English | Start in Month 1; build progressively |
| **P2** | Word usage, word swap, match-the-column, connectors | Seen in recent shift analyses; tests precise usage and relationships | Rotate into mixed practice |
| **P2** | Idioms, phrasal verbs, roots, spelling | Useful vocabulary support, but avoid isolated memorisation overload | Learn from actual context |
| **P3** | Rare/obscure grammar trivia and exhaustive word lists | Lower return unless repeated in PYQs or personally weak | Cover selectively |

**Priority rule:** P1 receives the most practice time. P2 is maintained through rotation. P3 is addressed only after core accuracy is stable or a fresh paper demonstrates a need.

## 4. Complete syllabus checklist

Use this as the master syllabus. A topic should be marked complete only after practice, error review, and at least one later revision.

### A. Reading comprehension and critical reading — P1

- [ ] Main idea / central theme
- [ ] Topic, title, and paragraph purpose
- [ ] Direct factual questions
- [ ] Inference and implied meaning
- [ ] Author's tone, attitude, and point of view
- [ ] Author's purpose and argument structure
- [ ] Strengthen/weaken or conclusion-based reasoning where relevant
- [ ] Vocabulary in context
- [ ] Phrase meaning and contextual substitution
- [ ] Reference words, pronouns, and antecedents
- [ ] Distinguishing fact, opinion, assumption, and conclusion
- [ ] Understanding contrast, concession, cause, effect, and examples
- [ ] Dense/editorial and economy, banking, technology, society, and policy passages

**Practice standard:** begin with careful untimed analysis; progress to timed passages. Aim for at least four RC passages per week once the foundation is in place. Review why every incorrect option is wrong.

### B. Core grammar — P1

- [ ] Parts of speech and functions in a sentence
- [ ] Nouns, pronouns, adjectives, and adverbs
- [ ] Subject–verb agreement, including collective/indefinite subjects
- [ ] Tenses and sequence of tenses
- [ ] Articles and determiners
- [ ] Prepositions and dependent prepositions
- [ ] Conjunctions and paired conjunctions
- [ ] Modals and auxiliary verbs
- [ ] Gerunds, infinitives, and participles
- [ ] Clauses: noun, adjective/relative, and adverb clauses
- [ ] Conditionals
- [ ] Active and passive voice
- [ ] Direct and indirect speech
- [ ] Comparisons and degrees
- [ ] Modifiers and misplaced/dangling modifiers
- [ ] Parallelism
- [ ] Pronoun reference and consistency
- [ ] Countable/uncountable nouns
- [ ] Redundancy, wordiness, and faulty construction
- [ ] Punctuation and sentence boundaries

**Application formats:** spotting errors, sentence correction, phrase replacement, sentence improvement, and paragraph error detection. Learn the rule, then solve questions that disguise the rule in context.

### C. Vocabulary and usage — P1/P2

- [ ] Meaning from context
- [ ] Synonyms and antonyms in context
- [ ] Collocations and dependent word combinations
- [ ] Confusing words and commonly misused pairs
- [ ] Word forms and parts-of-speech changes
- [ ] Idioms and phrases
- [ ] Phrasal verbs
- [ ] Prefixes, suffixes, and common roots
- [ ] One-word substitutions where supported by PYQs
- [ ] Spelling and commonly confused spellings
- [ ] Word usage in multiple sentences
- [ ] Word swap / word rearrangement
- [ ] Double and multiple fillers

**Rule:** learn fewer words deeply. For each useful word, record meaning, part of speech, a synonym/antonym if helpful, and one original sentence. Revisit it on the spaced-revision dates.

### D. Cloze and sentence-level verbal ability — P1

- [ ] Cloze tests: grammar-led and context-led
- [ ] Single and double fillers
- [ ] Sentence completion
- [ ] Phrase replacement
- [ ] Sentence improvement
- [ ] Connectors and linking expressions
- [ ] Match the columns
- [ ] Word swap / word rearrangement
- [ ] Sentence rearrangement
- [ ] Para-jumbles, including fixed first/last sentence formats
- [ ] Odd sentence out / paragraph completion
- [ ] Logical sequence and coherence

Focus on the relationship between ideas, not just memorised connector meanings.

### E. RBI Grade B descriptive writing — P1

**Essay**
- [ ] Understand the question's scope and command words
- [ ] Brainstorm and select relevant points
- [ ] Write a clear introduction and thesis
- [ ] Develop logically ordered body paragraphs
- [ ] Explain causes, effects, opportunities, risks, and trade-offs
- [ ] Use relevant examples, evidence, and balanced arguments
- [ ] Offer realistic solutions where appropriate
- [ ] Write a concise conclusion
- [ ] Avoid unsupported statistics, sweeping claims, and repetition

**Précis / summary**
- [ ] Identify the central idea and supporting points
- [ ] Separate essential ideas from examples and repetition
- [ ] Preserve the author's meaning and logical order
- [ ] Write in your own words
- [ ] Maintain an appropriate length as instructed
- [ ] Add a suitable title when required
- [ ] Check grammar, clarity, and factual fidelity

**Comprehension / analytical response**
- [ ] Read for argument, evidence, and implications
- [ ] Answer only what is asked
- [ ] Paraphrase accurately
- [ ] Support interpretation with the passage
- [ ] Avoid adding unrelated personal opinion

**General written expression**
- [ ] Paragraph unity and transitions
- [ ] Formal, natural tone
- [ ] Concise sentence construction
- [ ] Correct grammar, punctuation, and spelling
- [ ] Clear topic sentences
- [ ] Accurate use of examples and data
- [ ] Keyboard typing accuracy and speed

### F. SBI PO Mains descriptive communication — P1

The exact components must be checked against the applicable notification. The 2025 SBI PO notification listed email, report, and either situation analysis or précis.

- [ ] Professional email: purpose, subject, concise body, appropriate tone
- [ ] Report writing: title, context, findings, sequence, conclusion/recommendation
- [ ] Situation analysis: identify issue, stakeholders, options, risks, recommendation
- [ ] Précis: concise and faithful summary
- [ ] Typing under a strict time limit

## 5. Seven-month study roadmap

The month labels are sequence markers, not permission to ignore earlier skills. Continue RC, vocabulary, grammar revision, and writing throughout the plan.

### Month 1 — Foundation + diagnostic

**Learn**
- Parts of speech and sentence structure
- Subject–verb agreement
- Tenses
- Articles and determiners
- Prepositions and conjunctions
- Basic punctuation and sentence boundaries

**Do**
- Take one baseline objective English sectional test.
- Solve topic-wise PYQs after each concept.
- Start an error notebook and vocabulary bank.
- Write one short paragraph and one basic essay outline each week.

**Exit check:** explain the rule behind common errors and achieve at least 70% accuracy on fresh, topic-specific questions.

### Month 2 — Advanced grammar + correction

**Learn**
- Modifiers, parallelism, clauses, conditionals
- Gerunds/infinitives, voice, narration
- Pronoun reference, comparisons, redundancy

**Practise**
- Error detection, paragraph errors, phrase replacement
- Sentence improvement and mixed grammar PYQs
- Explain why each distractor is incorrect
- One timed descriptive task each week

**Exit check:** at least 75% accuracy on fresh mixed grammar sets, with recurring error types identified.

### Month 3 — Vocabulary + verbal ability

**Learn and practise**
- Contextual vocabulary, collocations, word forms
- Idioms, phrasal verbs, roots and affixes
- Cloze tests, fillers, word swap, match-the-column
- Connectors and sentence completion

**Writing**
- One essay plan and one short written response each week.
- Build a personal list of recurring vocabulary mistakes.

**Exit check:** solve mixed verbal-ability sets with improving accuracy and fewer context-based vocabulary errors.

### Month 4 — Reading comprehension + coherence

**Focus**
- Main idea, inference, tone, purpose, vocabulary in context
- Argument structure, fact vs opinion, assumptions
- Para-jumbles, sentence rearrangement, paragraph completion

**Weekly minimum**
- Four RC passages
- Two short timed verbal-ability sets
- One descriptive writing task
- One review of all RC mistakes

**Exit check:** explain the evidence for an answer, not merely identify the option.

### Month 5 — SBI PO objective English

**Focus**
- Prelims speed and accuracy
- Mains-level RC and paragraph reasoning
- Mixed PYQs and memory-based sets from multiple shifts/years
- Timed sectional tests

**Practice**
- At least two timed objective English sectionals per week, if the rest of the study schedule permits.
- Review time spent per question type.
- Practise skipping and returning rather than getting trapped in one question.

**Exit check:** stable performance across multiple fresh timed sets, not one unusually easy test.

### Month 6 — RBI writing + SBI Mains integration

**Focus**
- Essay planning and full essays
- Précis and summary writing
- Comprehension responses
- SBI email, report, and situation-analysis formats as required by the current notification
- Clear, concise, formal expression

**Practice**
- Two timed writing tasks per week.
- Type answers on a keyboard.
- Review content relevance, structure, coherence, grammar, and word economy.
- Build a theme bank: economy, banking, finance, inclusion, technology, AI, environment, education, health, society, governance, and public policy.

**Exit check:** complete a writing task within the prescribed time and self-evaluate it using the rubric below.

### Month 7 — Consolidation + exam simulation

**Focus**
- Reattempt incorrect and guessed PYQs
- Mixed objective sets and timed sectionals
- RC speed and inference accuracy
- Full descriptive practice under time limits
- Vocabulary, grammar, and error-notebook revision

**Weekly**
- Two objective sectionals or equivalent mixed timed sets
- Two descriptive tasks
- One cumulative revision session
- One weekly review and next-week adjustment

**Exit check:** consistent results across several fresh timed sets and complete written responses, with no major recurring error left unaddressed.

## 6. Daily 60-minute operating routine

Use this default session:

| Time | Task | What counts as completion |
|---|---|---|
| 10 min | Spaced revision | Reattempt due questions/rules/words; record result |
| 25 min | PYQs or exam-level questions | Attempt a defined set with source and timer |
| 15 min | Concept learning + corrections | Learn only the rule/approach exposed by practice |
| 10 min | Vocabulary / editorial reading | Record useful words or summarise one argument |

### If the day has a specific focus

- **Grammar day:** 10 min revision + 25 min topic PYQs + 15 min rule/correction + 10 min vocabulary.
- **RC day:** 10 min revision + 25 min timed passage + 15 min option analysis + 10 min vocabulary.
- **Writing day:** 10 min revision + 25 min timed writing + 15 min rubric-based review + 10 min vocabulary/reading.
- **Mock day:** use the 25-minute practice block for a timed set; use the remaining blocks to review it. Do not take a mock and skip analysis.

Never spend the entire hour passively watching lectures or reading grammar notes.

## 8. PYQ-first study process

Use this repeatable loop for every topic:

1. **Diagnose:** attempt 10–15 exam-level questions before studying the topic, where practical.
2. **Classify:** label each miss: concept gap, vocabulary gap, misread, logic gap, time pressure, careless error, or guess.
3. **Learn:** study only the concepts needed to fix the errors. Use a trusted explanation or grammar reference.
4. **Practise:** solve a fresh topic-wise set. Do not simply memorise the answers from the diagnostic set.
5. **Explain:** write a one-line reason for every wrong answer and every lucky guess.
6. **Time:** repeat with a timer once basic accuracy is stable.
7. **Revisit:** schedule D+1, D+7, D+15, and D+30 revision.

### Topic completion standard

A topic is **not complete** because a lecture was watched or notes were read.

Mark it complete only when:
- the core concept is understood;
- at least one topic-wise PYQ/exam-level set has been attempted;
- mistakes and guesses have been reviewed;
- a fresh-question reattempt shows acceptable accuracy;
- at least one scheduled revision has been completed.

## 9. Revision and accuracy rules

### Spaced revision schedule

| Review | When | Task |
|---|---|---|
| D+1 | Next day | Recall the rule; redo marked questions |
| D+7 | One week later | Solve a short fresh/mixed set |
| D+15 | Fifteen days later | Test retention without notes |
| D+30 | One month later | Cumulative check; move to maintenance if stable |

### Accuracy thresholds

| Result on fresh questions | Action |
|---|---|
| Below 70% | Relearn the concept; schedule another practice set within 2–3 days |
| 70–85% | Continue the D+1 / D+7 / D+15 / D+30 cycle |
| Above 85% twice on fresh sets | Move to maintenance; include in weekly mixed practice |

These are course-management thresholds, not official exam cut-offs. For timed sets, track both accuracy and time. High accuracy with very slow solving still needs speed work.

### Error categories

- **Concept gap:** rule or method not known.
- **Application gap:** knew the rule but could not apply it in context.
- **Vocabulary gap:** meaning, collocation, or word form unknown.
- **Logic gap:** missed the relationship between sentences/ideas.
- **Misread:** overlooked a qualifier, negative, or instruction.
- **Careless error:** avoidable marking or attention mistake.
- **Time pressure:** rushed or spent too long elsewhere.
- **Guess:** selected without sufficient evidence.

For each mistake, record: question/source, why the answer was wrong, correct rule or reasoning, and next reattempt date.

## 10. Descriptive writing evaluation rubric

Score each practice response out of 10. Use the same rubric each time so improvement is measurable.

| Dimension | Marks | What to check |
|---|---:|---|
| Relevance and content | 0–2 | Answers the actual prompt; accurate and sufficiently developed |
| Structure and coherence | 0–2 | Clear introduction, logical paragraphs, smooth transitions, conclusion |
| Analysis and depth | 0–2 | Explains causes, effects, trade-offs, examples, or implications |
| Language and grammar | 0–2 | Correct, precise, natural, formal English |
| Concision and presentation | 0–2 | No repetition, readable sentences, follows word/time instructions |

After scoring, write only the **three highest-impact corrections** for the next response. Do not rewrite everything without identifying the underlying weakness.

### Daily note workflow

- For every study day with reported/verified activity, create or update exactly one file: `logs/YYYY-MM-DD.md`.
- Use `templates/DAILY-LOG.md` as the structure. Record concepts learned, PYQ/practice results and sources, mistake explanations, vocabulary, revisions, descriptive-writing feedback (if applicable), and the next-session target.
- If multiple chats happen on the same date, append to that date's existing note; never create duplicate files for one day.
- Do not create a daily study note when no session data was supplied. Do not invent a session or fill unknown fields with guesses.

### Data integrity rules

- Record only sessions and results actually reported or supported by a study record.
- Never invent study time, question counts, accuracy, completion, or vocabulary learned.
- Keep question source details where available: exam, year, shift, platform/book, and question reference.
- Separate **attempted**, **correct**, **incorrect**, and **unattempted**.
- Accuracy = correct ÷ attempted × 100. Do not count unattempted questions in the denominator.
- A guessed correct answer should still be logged for review.
- Update the dashboard only from verified tracker/log data.
- When no session data is supplied, leave progress unchanged and do not create a fake daily study note. If multiple chats happen on the same date, append to that date's existing log rather than creating duplicates.

### Weekly review questions

1. How many study sessions and minutes were completed?
2. How many PYQs were attempted, and what was the accuracy?
3. Which three topics caused the most errors?
4. Which mistakes repeated from earlier weeks?
5. Which revisions were due, completed, or missed?
6. What was the descriptive-writing score and main weakness?
7. What are the three highest-priority actions for next week?

## 12. Resources and how to use them

Use a small number of resources consistently. Do not keep switching platforms.

- **PYQs and memory-based papers:** primary source for question style and trend tracking. Prefer complete shift-wise sets where available; label memory-based content as such.
- **S. P. Bakshi — Objective General English:** reference for grammar and objective practice. Use selectively to repair a concept, not as a book to read cover to cover before attempting questions.
- **The Hindu / Indian Express editorials:** reading practice and argument analysis. Read for thesis, evidence, tone, and structure; do not copy entire editorials into notes.
- **Word Power Made Easy:** optional vocabulary support. Prioritise words encountered in passages and PYQs.
- **Banking practice platforms:** Oliveboard, PracticeMock, Guidely, and BankersAdda can provide sectionals and memory-based sets. Check the year, exam, and shift before logging a question.
- **RBI website:** use official notifications, handouts, reports, and publications for RBI exam requirements and writing themes.

A resource is useful only if it produces practice, correction, or measurable improvement.

## 13. Monthly review and plan adjustment

At the end of each month:
- compare accuracy on fresh sets, not repeated questions alone;
- identify the highest-frequency mistake categories;
- check whether RC and writing have been practised consistently;
- move mastered topics into maintenance;
- allocate extra time to topics below 70% accuracy;
- revise the next month's focus if actual PYQs show a meaningful change.

Do not change priorities because of one viral exam analysis. Require repeated evidence across multiple shifts or years, and distinguish official pattern changes from memory-based topic reports.

## 15. Non-negotiable rules

- PYQs and exam-level questions are the centre of preparation.
- Every wrong answer and lucky guess must teach a rule or strategy.
- RC practice must include option analysis, not just answer checking.
- Vocabulary must be learned in context and revised.
- Descriptive writing starts early and is practised on a keyboard.
- Timed practice is introduced progressively; speed must not destroy accuracy.
- Trends inform priorities but never replace the official notification.
- No progress is recorded without evidence.
- The learner's actual weak areas override generic topic rankings.

**The weekly objective:** become measurably better at answering unseen questions and expressing ideas clearly under time pressure.
